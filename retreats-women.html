You have not slept properly in years.

And it is not the children keeping you awake. It is that the house finally goes quiet, everything you carried all day sits up and starts talking, and there is no one in the dark to hand it to.

12 seats · Deposit €400

You are the one everyone leans on, and you have been for so long that nobody thinks to ask any more. You answer the messages, you hold the calendar, you absorb the moods of an entire household and adjust yourself around them a hundred times a day without registering that you are doing it. Then at two in the morning you lie there with a weight sitting flat on your chest and you cannot name it, cannot move it, and would not know who to call about it if you could.

You snap at the people you love most over something that did not deserve it, and you spend the next hour quietly convicting yourself for it. You have caught your own mother's sentence coming out of your mouth and gone cold. And somewhere underneath all of it there is a thought you have never finished out loud: that you would like, just once, to let yourself fall — and that you have no earthly idea who would catch you.

You are not broken, and you are not too much. You are a woman who has been holding something far too heavy, entirely alone, for far too long — and holding it well is precisely why nobody has come to help.

The hands you land in
You are held by a licensed holistic therapist.

This is not a programme you are slotted into. There is no eight-module framework waiting here that you have to fold yourself down to fit, and no version of you that would be easier to work with than the one arriving. You have spent years making yourself the right shape for rooms that were never built with you in mind. That is a large part of what put the weight on your chest in the first place.

Twelve seats, and not one more, because twelve is the number of women I can genuinely hold in mind between one Tuesday and the next — your history, your body, the particular way you carry it, what happened in week three that you are still turning over. The work is built around that, week by week, in the room and in the messages between.

How the twelve weeks move
Phase 01 · Weeks 1–4

Say the thing you have never said

The betrayal you never received an apology for and stopped waiting on. The resentment toward your own children that there is no acceptable place to put. The control you call organisation. You will say it out loud, in a room where not one woman flinches or reaches over to fix you — and the ceiling will not come down. Most of what you are frightened of is the saying, not the thing itself.

Phase 02 · Weeks 5–8

Let yourself fall

Insight is not the problem. You understand your patterns better than anyone in your life does and it has not moved a single one of them, because they do not live in your understanding — they live in your jaw, your shoulders, the top third of your breath. So this is where it comes out of the body. You stop managing yourself for an hour a week and let somebody hold the other end. The chest opens. The breath goes lower. The nights get quieter first.

Phase 03 · Weeks 9–12

Keep your head up

Every other time you felt better, it held for a fortnight and then the old shape came back and took you with it. This is the part we build for. Trust rebuilt in the only order it can be — yourself first, then the people close enough to feel the difference. What you leave with has to work at half past seven on a Tuesday, with the school bags and the shouting. Anything that only works in the quiet room was never going to save you.

Twelve weeks from now
You feel safe inside yourself.

Not fixed, not finished, not a different woman. Simply no longer at war with the body you live in, and no longer doing it by yourself.

  • You sleep through the night, and you stop dreading the hour before bed.
  • The weight on your chest has moved, and you can tell the day it did.
  • You breathe all the way down again, without being reminded to.
  • The control loosens at the root instead of being managed for another decade.
  • You know what to do when it rises, with no retreat to escape to.
  • You are in the photograph with your children instead of behind the camera.
  • Eleven women know exactly what this cost you, and they are still there in week thirteen.
